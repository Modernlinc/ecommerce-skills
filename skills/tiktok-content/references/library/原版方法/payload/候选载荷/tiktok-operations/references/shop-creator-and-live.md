# TikTok Shop, creator, affiliate, and LIVE operations

This reference keeps the shared commerce, fulfillment, customer-service, creator, and LIVE admission gates. Use [shop-merchandising-and-health.md](shop-merchandising-and-health.md), [creator-collaboration-objects.md](creator-collaboration-objects.md), and [live-operations-and-analysis.md](live-operations-and-analysis.md) for deeper execution. Those references do not weaken the approval or safety gates here.

## Shop readiness gate

Confirm the target market's current official rules for:

- seller/entity and account eligibility;
- product category and required qualifications;
- warehouse, shipping, dispatch, returns, and customer service;
- listing/title/image/variation/claim requirements;
- creator/affiliate qualification and collaboration types;
- promotion availability and stacking;
- LIVE and shoppable-content eligibility.

Do not copy a US or Southeast Asia workflow into another market without rechecking it.

For registration preparation, use the user-specified market and branch; if either is missing, mark it not provided without choosing it for the user. Map only roles actually named by applicable, admitted sources; do not impose a universal applicant/owner/UBO/representative checklist. If role applicability lacks admitted evidence, keep it unknown rather than creating mandatory fields. For a source-required role, distinguish: required information not provided; non-applicability supported by applicable evidence, with its locator; and applicable role whose relationship to another role is unknown. Do not merge roles or transfer document or identifier alternatives across them without evidence. Seek only the minimum non-sensitive clarification needed for the next decision; continue unaffected offline planning and pause only the dependent eligibility conclusion or action. Record information categories, not private identity, tax or bank values. Field checks or submission do not establish approval, category eligibility or account availability.

## Product decision card

```text
Product and variant:
Target market and audience:
Demonstrability:
Distinctive value:
Problem/use case:
Proof and claims:
Content angles:
Listing readiness:
Package dimensions and weight:
Supply price, available stock, and replenishment capacity:
Dropship/warehouse route and order-to-dispatch time:
Brand ownership or authorization:
Category qualifications and product certifications:
Comparable offers and price band:
Existing channel evidence and return reasons:
Target-market service language and owner:
Cross-border return and after-sales route:
Full contribution economics:
Fulfillment/return risk:
Policy/qualification risk:
Smallest validation test:
Stop condition:
```

Treat “TikTok-able,” low price, viral examples, and third-party GMV as hypotheses. A product must also survive margin, fulfillment, claims, customer experience, and policy checks.

Use the card as an admission gate, not a viral-product score. Mark unsupported fields `unknown`; do not silently award a pass because the supplier or course omits packaging, authorization, stock, return, service, or cost evidence. A field can be not applicable only when the market, category, and operating route make that explicit.

Build an opportunity sheet with distinct evidence columns: first-party opportunity/search data, third-party trend estimates, competitor channel mix, comment/VOC signals, supply and fulfillment facts, contribution economics, and policy eligibility. Record market, window, and source. Two tools repeating the same estimate are not two independent proofs, and no fixed growth, listing-age, sales, or concentration threshold is universal.

For seasonal or event-led products, reverse-plan the purchase/production lead time, inbound and warehouse arrival, listing readiness, content warm-up, selling window, inventory-exit point, and after-sales tail. A trend spike is not an approval to buy inventory when the arrival or exit window is unknown.

Only create product variations for real buyer-receivable differences such as color, size, material, quantity, or compatible bundles. Keep image, title, price, stock, and description accurate for every option. Do not clone a product, fabricate a variant, or create a cheap unavailable option to manipulate distribution or displayed price.

## Contribution economics

Use the user's real definitions. At minimum consider:

```text
net revenue after discounts/refunds
- product cost
- inbound/domestic/international fulfillment
- platform/payment/tax costs
- creator commission and sample cost
- ad cost
- returns, chargebacks, and support cost
= retained contribution profit
```

Do not infer profitability from GMV or dashboard ROI alone.

Before using a pricing or promotion spreadsheet, audit its definitions, formula references, division direction, currency, tax/fee dates, refund maturity, and whether "margin" is calculated against revenue rather than cost. A course template is a field checklist, not an approved calculator.

For stacked promotions, write the actual post-stack transaction price and seller-funded amount for each layer—platform event, product discount, coupon, shipping, creator commission, and ads—and stop if the worst permitted stack exceeds the approved contribution boundary or fulfillment capacity.

## Choose a selling path

Select the route from product evidence and operating capacity, not from a course's preferred tactic:

| Selling path | Best used when | Evidence needed before expansion | Common failure to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| owned shoppable content | the product can be demonstrated clearly and the team can sustain original sales content | qualified product views, checkout behavior, retained orders, repeatable creative variables | views without product understanding or listing mismatch |
| creator/affiliate | a creator's audience and presentation add trust or reach the shop cannot create alone | audience fit, compliant delivery, tracked sample/fee/commission, content rights, refunds and contribution result | choosing by follower count, uncontrolled claims, unclear paid-use rights |
| LIVE | demonstration, comparison, objection handling, bundles, or urgency genuinely improve the decision | qualified room traffic, product clicks, conversion, fulfillment capacity, moderation and post-LIVE contribution | discount-driven orders that create stock, service, refund, or margin failure |
| Product GMV Max | selected eligible products, listing, creative rights, fulfillment, a non-LIVE baseline, attribution window and break-even boundary already exist | selected-product non-LIVE GMV, status/window, organic/affiliate overlap, contribution economics and an approved loss ceiling | mixing LIVE-room orders into Product, or reading platform ROI as incremental profit |
| LIVE GMV Max | a selected eligible LIVE event, host/moderation, product/Shop scope, rights, inventory/fulfillment, in-room conversion definition and break-even boundary already exist | selected liveroom, in-room/all-shops conversion readback, post-exit exclusion, object lifecycle, contribution economics and an approved loss ceiling | merging Product and LIVE totals, ignoring auto-paused objects, or assuming an automatic restart |
| coordinated portfolio | different channels have explicit roles and the team can trace costs, rights, and handoffs | channel-level role, owner, asset authorization, inventory capacity and a common profit definition | every channel changing at once, double-counted attribution, no causal learning |

The smallest useful sequence is usually: establish product/offer and economics, obtain one credible proof path, test one primary channel, repair the first broken conversion layer, and add another channel only when it answers a defined business question. Do not default to running content, creators, LIVE, and ads simultaneously.

For paid Shop delivery, route Product GMV Max and LIVE GMV Max to the two separate object cards and shared same-window reconciliation in [ads-measurement-and-experiments.md](ads-measurement-and-experiments.md). A combined “GMV Max” label is not enough to select, measure, deduplicate, pause, or restart either object.

Before choosing the primary path, state which commerce field must do the first proof work:

- **Shop Tab/search** needs accurate categorization, searchable product language, attributes, comparable value, listing proof, inventory, and fulfillment certainty.
- **owned shoppable content** needs a visually or narratively demonstrable problem, use case, difference, or result that the product can substantiate.
- **creator/affiliate** needs audience and presentation fit plus controlled claims, sample economics, deliverables, disclosure, and usage rights.
- **LIVE** needs a reason that demonstration, comparison, objection handling, bundles, or interaction materially improves the buying decision.

The same product can participate in several fields, but each added field must have a defined job and evidence threshold. A generic “hot product list” is not a channel strategy.

## Creator/affiliate workflow

1. confirm shop and product qualification;
2. define creator-audience/product match and prohibited claims;
3. choose the current supported collaboration type;
4. agree deliverables, timing, commission/fee, sample, disclosure, usage rights, revision, cancellation, and dispute terms;
5. track sample and content status;
6. verify the published link and product match;
7. record organic and paid-use authorization separately;
8. review orders, refunds, contribution economics, content quality, and relationship outcome;
9. continue, revise, hold, or stop.

### Creator tracker fields

Creator/account, market/language, audience evidence, product, collaboration type, fee/commission, sample, deadline, content status, post URL, disclosure, content-rights scope, paid-use/Spark authorization, expiry, spend approval, performance window, orders/refunds, contribution result, issue, next action.

Do not require Spark authorization from every creator. Ask transparently, record scope and duration, respect refusal, and use only the authorization actually granted.

## Customer-service operating loop

Treat customer service as part of the operating system rather than a punishment-avoidance tactic:

1. define roles, markets/languages, working windows, and minimum necessary permissions;
2. build FAQ and response candidates from actual pre-sale, order, logistics, return, refund, and complaint questions;
3. let automation draft or route routine cases, while a named human owns exceptions, sensitive data, refunds, disputes, and policy-sensitive claims;
4. log case type, promised action, owner, deadline, resolution, repeat cause, and product/content feedback;
5. feed repeated questions and complaints back into listings, creator briefs, fulfillment, and product decisions.

Do not assume universal 24/7 staffing, response-time penalties, or account consequences from course claims. Verify current market rules and service-level requirements before turning them into staffing or automation policy. Never expose more order or customer data than the task requires.

For proactive or automated customer marketing, document the audience trigger, consent basis, message purpose, frequency cap, suppression/opt-out behavior, owner, and service escalation. “Set and forget” is not an acceptable operating state.

## Fulfillment route and exception control

Choose self-fulfillment, a domestic/cloud warehouse, a third-party overseas warehouse, or an integration by comparing order volume, total landed cost, dispatch and delivery needs, inventory visibility, return handling, compliance, and operational control. Do not select a route from one course's fixed price or speed claim.

Before relying on an ERP or third-party fulfillment connection:

1. map product and variant SKUs;
2. verify inventory ownership and sync direction;
3. run a controlled test order and cancellation/return scenario;
4. assign an owner for failed import, label, dispatch, tracking, stock, refund, and duplicate-order exceptions;
5. preserve evidence of the correction and feed recurring faults back into the route decision.

## LIVE plan

Create a run-of-show with:

- audience promise and eligible product set;
- host roles, demo order, proof, FAQ, moderation, and service escalation;
- accurate offer, inventory, fulfillment, and disclosure cues;
- technical rehearsal, backup assets, stop conditions, and post-LIVE review;
- separate organic LIVE and paid LIVE measurement where the platform permits it.

Going LIVE, changing giveaways, promotions, products, or inventory requires explicit approval.
